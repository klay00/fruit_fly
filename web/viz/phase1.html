Fly to Dragon · Phase 1 gate

Sugar reaches the proboscis in fifty milliseconds

Twenty sugar-sensing neurons are stimulated on the right side of a fruit fly's brain. Nothing else is touched. Fifty milliseconds later, two synapses downstream, the motor neuron that extends the proboscis begins to fire — carried there entirely by 54.5 million measured synapses. Below is one second of that, spike by spike.

What this is not

  • This is neural dynamics, not consciousness. Whether insects have subjective experience is an open and contested question. Nothing here addresses it.
  • The violet marks are ours, not the brain's. They are the Poisson current we inject into the sugar neurons. Only the teal and amber spikes were generated by the connectome.
  • MN9 activity is a proxy. It is the model's stand-in for proboscis extension, not a measurement of a real fly's behaviour.
  • Firing rates are not calibrated. They scale with a free parameter (0.275 mV per synapse) that neither we nor the original authors tuned against recordings.
